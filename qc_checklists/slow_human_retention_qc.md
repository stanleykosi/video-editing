# Slow Human Retention QC

Use this checklist for slower, human short-form edits where connection,
imperfection, proof, warmth, and contrast carry retention more than constant
hyper-editing.

## Hook And Versioning

- First 3-5 seconds contain motion, proof, face, or a clear reason to keep watching.
- Opening dynamic zoom or image stack preserves face, captions, proof, and action.
- Final cut was selected from named timeline versions or compared variants.
- The chosen version is balanced for story and connection, not only shortest duration.

## Human Rhythm

- Slow sections have a named emotional, proof, story, or comprehension purpose.
- Kept imperfections create warmth, humor, vulnerability, or character.
- Any bystander or unscripted human moment has no consent, privacy, or dignity risk.
- Pattern interrupt exits before the main story loses momentum.
- Short-form black/drop gaps stay under one second unless intentionally cinematic.

## Motion And Proof

- Face, eyes, or emotional subject stay guided through early cuts and zooms.
- Proof artifacts appear close to the line they support.
- Long dynamic zooms stay crop-safe and do not drift away from the intended focus.
- The edit does not add effects only because a section feels long.

## Sound

- Foley matches visible or strongly implied movement.
- Foley, risers, whooshes, and reverb remain below dialogue.
- Contrast drops land after a clear audible or visual build.
- Audio edges around micro drops are faded and do not click.

## Captions And Color

- Captions support comprehension without covering faces, proof, or human reactions.
- Caption style is readable but not overly digital for the intended tone.
- Warm grade supports hope or memory while preserving believable skin.
- Glow, grain, radial blur, and vignette do not reduce caption/proof readability.
