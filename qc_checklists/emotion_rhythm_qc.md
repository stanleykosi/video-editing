# Emotion And Rhythm QC

Use this checklist when a cut depends on performance, emotional belief, invisible
rhythm, or intentional discomfort.

## Emotional Readability

- The eyes, face, or body reaction that carries the emotion is visible at delivery size.
- The edit does not cut away before the viewer can register the emotional change.
- Silence is removed only when it is truly empty, not when it is carrying thought or feeling.
- Major realizations, failures, or consequences have enough post-beat time to land.
- Kept human imperfections create connection rather than accidental sloppiness.
- Short-form human interruptions return to the story before momentum dies.

## Rhythm

- Natural rhythm cuts land on a readable cue: breath, gesture, glance, step, line ending, or environmental cycle.
- Audio ambience and room tone stay smooth across invisible cuts.
- Shot matching, color, and motion do not make an otherwise natural cut feel accidental.
- The cut still feels right when watched cold after looping the edit point.

## Build And Release

- Climactic sequences have a visible build, peak, and release.
- Shot durations compress toward the peak only when that supports the emotion.
- Post-peak release is not trimmed so tightly that the consequence feels unearned.
- Music, sound effects, and motion settle when the scene needs aftermath.

## Intentional Discomfort

- Any jarring cut has a named emotional purpose.
- The unusual timing is rare enough to feel intentional.
- The cut creates the desired reaction rather than confusion about continuity.
- A normal rhythm version was considered or tested for comparison.
- Any black/drop gap in short-form stays under one second unless intentionally cinematic.

## Captions, Motion, Sound, Color

- Captions do not cover eyes, mouth, reactions, or the emotional subject.
- Caption animation does not steal attention during quiet holds.
- Motion graphics remain secondary during subtle performance beats.
- Sound design supports emotional timing without forcing the scene forward.
- Color preserves eye detail, facial contrast, and skin tone.
