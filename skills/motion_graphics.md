# Motion Graphics

## Purpose

Guide overlays, callouts, diagrams, UI motion, title systems, maps, lower thirds,
transitions, and explainer graphics.

## When To Use This Skill

- Adding visual explanations or emphasis.
- Building reusable animated overlays.
- Designing title cards, maps, lower thirds, captions-adjacent graphics, or visual aids.
- Using HyperFrames or Remotion for professional title/motion graphics with user
  approval frames before final compositing.
- Translating tutorials into Remotion, Blender, Manim, or ffmpeg compositions.
- Checking whether a graphic style matches the subject tone.
- Separating grounded reality from fantasy, promotional footage, proof, or imagined futures through visual treatment.
- Using keyframed zooms, overlays, arrows, text, and background treatment to direct attention in tutorials or shorts.
- Building short-form visual stacks that turn spoken concepts into timed text, icons, stock footage, GIFs, masks, glows, and transitions.
- Building Premiere short-form hook text, filler background cards, B-roll text sequences, full-screen object animations, and adjustment-layer zoom polish.
- Building Resolve Fusion motion with Transform nodes, Spline easing, Text+, overlays, and flash transitions.
- Building After Effects Reel scenes with eye-locked zooms, text presets, track mattes, null/camera moves, path counters, and branded background textures.
- Building scripted YouTube visual stacks with sketches, simple-tool animations, Premiere text pops, and texture overlays.
- Building slow human short-form motion where simple dynamic zooms, proof frames, and restrained captions carry attention.
- Building editorial documentary graphics with paper backgrounds, maps, charts, document highlights, screenshot focus sequences, cutouts, and low-FPS texture.
- Designing typographic hierarchy, live-action text placement, font roles, line breaks, and spacing for motion graphics.
- Building Resolve Fusion node composites, tracked graphics, Magic Mask effects,
  and simple Edit page keyframed titles.
- Building premium branded intros with logo, proof, outcome text, flicker, underline, and optional 3D/Fusion camera movement.
- Translating CapCut keyframes into custom overlay paths, zooms, tilts, route markers, and cutout/object animations.
- Translating CapCut property keyframes into exact focus zooms, sticker paths,
  compound-wrapper animations, and filter reveals without unwanted property
  changes.
- Building stylized CapCut/anime motion with character masks, glow, shadow, flicker, turbulent transitions, wide-angle distortion, and effect-tail extensions.
- Building CapCut anime effects with aligned eye/power overlays and Pendulum movement passes.
- Shaping graph curves and easing for CapCut-style keyframe motion and intense zoom stacks.
- Turning still images into cinematic moving scenes with motivated overlays,
  Screen blend modes, soft masks, localized light, and unifying grades.
- Building CapCut-style compositing tricks such as text behind people, chroma
  video-in-text, tracked stickers, photo stacks, chapter cards, PiP borders,
  split-mask illusions, clone effects, and green-screen VFX.
- Building CapCut viral text motion such as smooth slide-up text, axis stretch,
  opacity flicker, number counters, perspective freeze stacks, Player 3 caption
  compounds, highlight wipes, staggered blur reveals, gradient type, font-shift
  loops, outline reveals, and glow sweeps.
- Rebuilding CapCut-style typography as Remotion components or FFmpeg filter
  graphs for repeatable agent-controlled renders.
- Building CapCut documentary-style motion with switch-focus blur, research
  overlays, archival frames, filmed-screen texture, and parallax cutout collages.

## Core Principles

- Motion graphics should reduce cognitive load.
- Animate hierarchy: reveal what matters first.
- Keep labels and callouts near the thing they explain.
- Match timing to narration and viewer parsing speed.
- Graphics must match subject tone. Serious documentary usually needs restraint.
- Clever transitions, including match cuts, should mean something.
- Aspect bars, rotation, digital zoom, and movement direction should function as visual grammar, not decoration.
- Visual treatment can distinguish reality from fantasy only when the meaning is applied consistently.
- Every overlay needs a job: focus, proof, context, transition cover, speaker ID, or explanation.
- Keyframed focus moves should hold after they land so the viewer can read the target.
- One primary focal point should dominate each moment.
- Show, do not tell, when a visual representation can communicate the spoken idea faster than more narration.
- Dense visual stacks need hierarchy: primary idea first, support asset second, texture or background last.
- Subject tracking should keep the speaker centered while preserving room for captions and proof.
- Resolve/Fusion motion should be saved as a preset only after it is readable in vertical preview.
- Match visual effort to line value: filler cards for simple phrases, B-roll text for story/proof beats, and full-screen animations for high-value concepts.
- Reusable motion presets should be named by job and checked with captions, proof, and phone-safe crop.
- After Effects-heavy Reels need segment ideation before animation; each scene should know whether it needs captions only, speaker plus text, B-roll, icons, full-screen animation, or camera/path work.
- Editing signatures should stay coherent across fonts, colors, icon style, backgrounds, glows, textures, and transitions.
- Sketch complex animations before building them; choose the simplest tool that communicates the idea cleanly.
- Text and overlays should have jobs: comprehension, emphasis, scene attachment, highlight, transition, or mood.
- Slow human motion should feel intentional but not over-produced; use fewer moves when proof, face, or story is already carrying attention.
- Vox-inspired graphics should borrow the grammar of editorial explainers, not copy protected brand identity.
- Maps, charts, documents, and screenshot sequences must remain evidence-first after style, texture, and motion are added.
- Typography is a hierarchy system: pick the impact word, then use weight, hue, case, size, spacing, and motion to support it.
- Live-action text belongs in negative space or a deliberately tracked scene area, not on top of the focal point.
- Font family roles should match the project tone before motion polish begins.
- In Resolve, use Edit page inspector keyframes for simple title/position moves
  and Fusion only when the effect needs nodes, masks, tracking, or compositing.
- Branded intro motion should communicate brand identity, credible proof or authority, and viewer outcome in that order before becoming decorative.
- Dense intro text can use multiple layers only when each layer has a role: brand, proof, quote, keyword, underline, or transition.
- Keyframe-based motion should be chosen for custom path, timing, or focus control; use simpler preset animation when it already communicates cleanly.
- Scale and position should move together on focus zooms so the eyes, object, or
  proof target does not drift while the viewer is trying to read it.
- Compound wrappers are useful for unlocking animation controls on sticker/text
  layers, but only after the base graphic placement is stable.
- Social text motion should create a reading path. If the effect makes the word
  slower to understand, reduce the motion or use a simpler type treatment.
- Premium text motion should still be ordinary layer logic: duplicate text
  cleanly, compound only the needed layer, mask with clear start/end states, and
  keep a stable final word.
- Programmatic text motion should expose the same layer logic as data: source
  text, duplicate alignment, mask timing, glow pass, safe region, and final hold.
- Transparent overlays are motion-graphics assets, not just renders: alpha,
  codec, pixel format, timing, and edge behavior must be planned before handoff.
- Character-focused glow, shadow, and flicker should be isolated to the subject or body part carrying the beat.
- Dense effect stacks should be pre-rendered, nested, or isolated only after the underlying clip timing is stable enough to preserve editability.
- Small feature overlays need playback alignment; do not trust a paused still when the eye, symbol, or mask moves.
- Smooth graph curves make motion feel designed; flat or sharp curves should be chosen only when constant speed or hard impact is intentional.
- Still-image composites should animate the motion the image already implies;
  environmental overlays must match the plate before global polish is added.
- Mobile compositing effects need the same discipline as desktop effects: plan
  capture, choose layer order, review masks/keys/tracking across time, and keep
  licensed assets documented.
- Documentary depth effects should guide attention and source type; if blur,
  texture, or parallax makes evidence harder to inspect, simplify the scene.

## Professional Render Rules

- Use `skills/professional_title_graphics_pipeline.md` for title cards, hook
  cards, chapter cards, stat cards, lower thirds, and major typography overlays.
- HyperFrames is the default engine for title cards, hook cards, chapter cards,
  stat cards, lower thirds, HTML/CSS overlay systems, and transparent motion-card
  renders.
- Remotion is the default engine for polished motion graphics, animated
  typography, reusable components, data-driven graphics, and frame-precise social
  graphics.
- Final captions use ASS subtitles and should remain visible through motion
  graphics by default.
- Do not use Pillow/PIL for final title, caption, lower-third, hook-card, or
  chapter-card typography. Limit it to diagnostics, masks, contact sheets,
  placeholders, or non-typographic helper assets.
- New or materially changed title/motion graphics require approval frames/contact
  sheets and user approval before final compositing.
- Caption suppression is not a default motion-graphics solution. Reposition or
  restyle captions first; suppress only with explicit user approval.

## Timing Rules

- Hold maps, routes, and explanatory graphics until the viewer understands the information.
- Title cards can be useful after a strong long-form opening; in short videos they may waste time.
- Transitions should not steal time from emotional or evidentiary beats.
- Pause beats after treatment changes can be 1-2 seconds when the viewer needs to absorb scale or unreality.
- Proof screenshots, maps, and treatment-heavy footage must remain onscreen long enough to parse.
- Social overlay pop-ins can be 4-8 frames; calmer explainer overlays often need 8-16 frames.
- Focus zooms should land before or within 2 frames of the spoken reference or beat.
- Hold the zoomed or highlighted target until the viewer can understand it.
- Text or icon pop-ins in high-energy shorts can start around 4-8 frames when readability holds.
- Statement zooms should hold 8-18 frames after landing.
- After a dense hook, add a lower-density rest beat if the viewer needs to process the point.
- Resolve/Fusion B-roll zooms can start around Size 1.95 and animate to 1.0 only when vertical crop remains safe.
- Manual white flashes should peak around the cut and fade down within a few frames.
- Hook text design should establish its hierarchy within the first five seconds.
- Filler cards and B-roll text sequences should clear when their spoken concept ends.
- Adjustment zoom-blur moves should settle before captions, UI, product, or proof need to be read.
- After Effects text entrances should start on or just before the spoken concept and clear before the next segment competes.
- Camera/null movement can begin a few frames before the visible scene beat when a static scene start would feel dead.
- Path and counter animations should reach their final readable state before the spoken concept ends.
- Premiere text pop can use 70% scale, 110% overshoot about 7 frames later, then 100% settle about 5 frames after the overshoot.
- Animation reveals should follow the sketched order and voiceover timing.
- In slower human shorts, the opening dynamic zoom or proof/image stack should clarify the first 3-5 seconds.
- Long emotional sections can use one gentle dynamic zoom when additional effects would weaken the human focus.
- Map route and arrow reveals should complete before the viewer needs the destination.
- Bar chart wipes should settle long enough for data comparison.
- Document highlights and screenshot focus sequences should hold long enough to read source wording.
- Low-FPS/posterize texture belongs after timing lock.
- Typography hierarchy should be understandable before the graphic changes or the next claim begins.
- Display or script text needs slightly more read time than plain sans labels.
- Apple-style slide-up text can use about 22 frames as a premium starting point,
  then must settle before the word is read.
- Flicker and font-shift text should resolve to stable readability before the
  next caption, proof, or CTA competes.
- Number counters should slow or hold on the final verified value.
- Highlight, gradient, glow, and staggered reveal text should finish the readable
  state before the next visual claim competes.
- Remotion text presets should use Sequence-local timing and hold the final
  readable state after spring, mask, or glitch motion.
- FFmpeg text overlays should use explicit enable windows; long word chains should
  be split, converted to ASS, or moved to Remotion.
- Transparent overlay duration, fps, frame count, and start frame must match the
  intended edit window before the asset is imported into another timeline.
- In-place wipe reveals should animate the matte while the foreground remains
  fixed; use about 6-15 frames for fast social text or 12-24 frames for calmer
  title/lower-third overlays.
- Resolve Edit page title moves can start around 4-8 frames for fast social use
  or 8-16 frames for calmer use, then hold until readable.
- Premium social intros should usually finish around 3-5 seconds; podcast/show intros should usually finish around 10-15 seconds maximum.
- Intro flicker or font-change accents should stay in short 3-9 frame bursts and leave the final word readable.
- Resolve/Fusion 3D camera or logo moves should be kept around the readable beat rather than delaying brand/proof/outcome.
- In CapCut-style keyframing, closer keyframes create faster snaps and farther keyframes create slower motion; add a hold when the target must be read.
- A quick CapCut focus zoom can use about 4 frames between scale/position
  keyframes when the emphasis is intentional and the crop remains safe.
- Overlay paths should reach the callout/route target before the narration or visual reference has passed.
- Transition shakes should be tuned after turbulent and wide-angle layers are visible, then settle before the next action pose needs to be read.
- Pendulum movement should be added only after timing lock and reviewed with the final transition stack.
- Eye/power overlays should reveal or lock on the intended beat after speed adjustment.
- Effect-tail extensions should be trimmed to the beat and should not linger into the next focal point.
- Graph-shaped zooms should accelerate and settle according to the edit's job, then hold or clear before the viewer loses focus.
- Motion blur should be applied after timing lock and reduced when it harms readability.
- Still-image environmental overlays should loop or trim without visible pops
  and local flicker should start and end with the visible or implied light source.
- Subject-sandwich, chroma-key, and split-mask tricks should be approved after
  the baked base, cutout, key, and mask timing are stable.
- Slide-on photos and chapter cards should clear before the next focal beat or
  caption region competes.
- Switch-focus depth blur should hand off attention over a readable window, such
  as about 1s to 3s, with one subject sharp enough to read at all times.
- Documentary title animations around 1.5-2 seconds should settle before the
  viewer needs the word or source label.
- Research overlays and filmed-screen textures need holds when UI or source text
  functions as evidence.

## Motion Rules

- Avoid trendy wipes, goofy templates, and old channel effects when they clash with mature subject matter.
- Use restrained title, map, and lower-third motion for sensitive documentary.
- Match B-roll movement direction when motion continuity is the goal.
- Use match cuts only when the visual match adds story meaning.
- Use letterbox or aspect treatment only when it communicates a story world, source type, fantasy, or perspective.
- Use subtle rotation across a shot run only when it connects the shots into a shared motif and preserves readable edges.
- A digital zoom-out against forward camera motion can create an unstable parallax effect for unreal or overwhelming reveals.
- Use subtle 105-115% punch-ins for talking-head emphasis or jump-cut concealment before choosing aggressive zooms.
- Use stroke, shadow, backing boxes, background dim, or blur when footage competes with text readability.
- Do not let overlays, captions, title text, and action all compete for attention.
- Use zooms for important statements and tracking for subject movement.
- Use masks and feathered stock footage to blend support visuals without hiding the subject.
- Highlight only the meaning-changing word or symbol when using glow or accent color.
- In Fusion, use Transform nodes for size, position, and angle, then refine motion in Spline.
- Use Text+ for custom caption/title motion when basic text cannot provide enough control.
- For filler cards, centered text on flat or gradient backgrounds is enough when the phrase does not need proof footage.
- For B-roll text, use dimming or desaturation only enough to separate text from footage.
- For full-screen object animations, animate the main object first, then use support shapes and text as secondary hierarchy.
- For adjustment zooms, duplicate a tested adjustment layer only where section flow or emphasis needs it.
- Keep speaker eyes aligned during AE zooms by using a guide and animating scale plus position together.
- Parent text or cutouts only when they should share motion; remember opacity does not automatically follow child layers.
- Use nulls for grouped scene motion when direct keyframes would conflict with local layer animation.
- Convert only camera-responsive layers to 3D, and check that camera moves do not reveal background edges.
- Use track mattes with duplicate cutouts so texture adds style without erasing the original subject.
- Keep the point of focus consistent across adjacent zooms and cuts using guides, center, eyeline, or rule-of-thirds targets.
- Use simple exported animations for simple text/shape beats; use AE or Remotion when path, typewriter, glow, or diagram timing needs more control.
- Use Screen/Add-style texture overlays only for specific highlight, transition, or mood beats.
- For human pattern interrupts, crop or zoom only enough to make the reaction readable.
- Keep proof artifacts close to the spoken line they support.
- Warm paper/grid backgrounds should stay subordinate to foreground evidence.
- Compound or nest dense infographic scenes so scene-level moves do not desync internal layers.
- Avoid applying low-FPS texture to captions or dense labels unless readability is preserved.
- Use text motion after font, line break, hierarchy, spacing, and negative-space placement are working.
- Scene-attached typography should either remain still in clean whitespace or track smoothly enough to feel intentional.
- In Fusion, node order decides whether a correction affects one branch or the
  combined composite; review the result back in the timeline.
- Use tracker or Magic Mask effects only after checking drift, occlusion, edge chatter, and caption safety.
- Intro motion should move the eye through one primary focus at a time: brand/show name, proof or authority, then outcome.
- Limit font mixing to controlled roles; five useful font roles is already dense for a fast intro.
- Underlines, highlights, and flicker should mark impact words rather than animate every word equally.
- Axis stretch should affect one impact word and one axis at a time.
- Perspective freeze text should use the most readable angle, then remove unused
  animation tails.
- Player 3 captions, highlight wipes, gradient type, and glow sweeps should be
  used only after the base typography is already readable.
- Programmatic text masks and glow sweeps should be checked as layered overlays:
  base text, styled duplicate, animated mask, optional glow duplicate, and hold.
- Remotion glitch/scramble text should use seeded randomness and resolve to a
  stable word before the next focal beat.
- Programmatic alpha overlays should be checked as render assets: transparent
  background, alpha-capable output, mask polarity, edge behavior, and import
  behavior in the receiving tool.
- Procedural FFmpeg masks should be previewed alone before they control a
  foreground layer.
- For custom overlay paths, start from offscreen or route origin, land on the focus, hold, and clear before the next beat.
- For sticker/object paths, set size, flip, and rotation before keyframing
  position so the object follows the intended gaze, finger, route, or exit.
- For tilt tension, zoom first and then rotate so the frame never exposes black corners.
- For cutout/object animation, match the path style to tone: stepped movement for playful walk cycles, smooth movement for route or product glide.
- For character mask effects, check the full affected range for edge chatter, haloing, and glow spill.
- Use turbulent and wide-angle transition effects only where the distortion supports impact without making the subject unreadable.
- Use Pendulum movement as a controlled swing layer; stack passes only while the subject and next pose remain readable.
- Align eye/power overlays over the full motion range, then clear or cut before tracking drift becomes visible.
- When extending an effect tail, duplicated layers and backing clips should stay secondary to the foreground subject.
- Pair scale and position keyframes before graph shaping so the focal point does not drift.
- Do not stack heavy graph zoom passes unless the crop and source quality can survive the magnification.
- For still-image composites, place moving overlays only where the still image
  plausibly contains that motion, then resize, mask, feather, and blend before
  applying a final grade.
- Local strobe or flicker should affect only nearby reflected surfaces, not the
  entire frame, unless the whole scene is intentionally lit by that source.
- For subject-sandwich composites, place text/VFX between the base and the
  foreground cutout; for green-screen VFX, crop/key/grade the effect before
  judging the foreground layer.
- For locked-camera illusions, align split masks to real object edges and reject
  takes with auto exposure/focus changes.
- For switch-focus effects, compound foreground cutouts separately, ease the
  shared move, and use opposite blur ramps to move attention from one subject to
  another.
- For parallax documentary collages, reduce copied background motion so
  foreground, background, and text do not slide as one flat layer.
- Filmed-screen textures should create camera realism without hiding cursor
  action, UI, source text, or captions.

## Sound Rules

- Motion graphics with SFX should not overpower dialogue or testimony.
- Avoid whooshes and hits that make serious documentary graphics feel cheap.
- Add a distinct sound beat when a strong image intentionally breaks movement continuity.
- Pair SFX with actual overlay, zoom, shake, or reveal frames; avoid repetitive whooshes for every graphic.
- Reverse hits can build anticipation into a question, reveal, or transition object when the visual actually pays it off.
- Add flash or zoom SFX only when they support the motion and do not mask speech.
- Pair SFX with hook text, object entrances, or zooms only when the motion has a real emphasis job.
- Add SFX after AE render alignment so hits, whooshes, typewriter cues, and camera sounds can land on visible motion frames.
- Pair text pops and texture overlays with SFX only when the visual beat matters.
- Pair ticks/clicks with flicker, font shifts, or number counters only when the
  cue lands on visible changes and clears with the animation.
- Pair glitch or shimmer cues with staggered or glow text only when the cue lands
  on the visible reveal and clears before the next spoken phrase.
- Slow human visual beats often need natural ambience or subtle foley more than loud whooshes.
- Infographic SFX should cue meaningful reveals, highlights, route movement, chart bars, or screenshot swaps.
- Fusion title, tracker, or composite SFX should land only on intentional reveal
  or emphasis beats.
- Intro logo, text, underline, flicker, and camera cues should share frame markers with SFX so the audio feels intentional.
- SFX should be added only after the keyframed path or object motion is stable enough to place cues on real movement frames.
- Sticker, text, or compound-wrapper animation SFX should land on the actual
  entrance, reveal, or exit frame rather than the source layer's unwrapped start.
- AMV/action transition SFX should land on the actual shake, impact, or distortion peak, not just near the cut.
- Eye/power overlay SFX should land on the reveal or lock frame, not merely near the clip start.
- Still-image composites often need subtle ambience, such as fire, wind, rain, or
  room tone, more than hits; keep it below narration and document asset rights.
- Clone, VFX, and tracked-sticker effects can use foley, hits, or pops only when
  the cue lands on a visible event and stays below dialogue.
- Research timelapse risers should be restrained and should resolve into a real
  discovery, proof frame, or claim.

## Caption Rules

- Lower thirds should not block subtitles needed for story comprehension.
- Text and graphics should not overlap faces, maps, evidence, or key action.
- Captions should not collide with letterbox bars, proof screenshots, maps, or treatment boundaries.
- Overlay text, lower thirds, and captions should be designed together as one focus stack.
- Short-form captions must not cover title text, proof cards, faces, or the action being explained.
- Reposition or simplify captions when visual text already communicates the spoken concept; suppress only with explicit user approval.
- Text+ stacks should not duplicate auto captions unless they are the intentional emphasis layer.
- Keep captions visible or repositioned when hook text, B-roll text, filler cards, or full-screen hierarchy text already carries the spoken idea; suppress only with explicit user approval.
- Fade captions out before dense AE animation sections instead of cutting them harshly.
- Do not duplicate captions with center text or scene-attached text unless accessibility requires it.
- Use restrained caption motion or soft fades when kinetic captions would make an organic section feel too digital.
- Captions and graphic text should share a planned hierarchy; do not let both compete for the same impact word.
- Keep ordinary captions visible or repositioned during designed intro text when brand, proof, or outcome already carries the spoken words; suppress only with explicit user approval.
- Avoid placing captions on flickering reflections, fire, or moving sky when the
  moving layer changes contrast frame to frame.
- Captions should avoid subject cutout edges, PiP frames, tracked stickers,
  chroma-key titles, and VFX impact regions; any suppression requires explicit
  user approval.
- Captions should avoid eye/power-overlay transformation beats when the face is the visual focus.
- Captions should avoid switch-focus targets, film-frame borders, dense research
  overlays, filmed-screen UI, and parallax title/cutout intersections.

## Color Rules

- Graphic colors should fit the project tone and remain readable.
- Do not use bright, playful graphics over grief, war, crisis, or testimony unless the contrast is deliberate and approved.
- Reality/fantasy treatment should preserve evidence detail, readable geography, and facial/body-language cues.
- Use accent color on the word or icon that changes meaning; keep texture overlays subordinate.
- Gradient text must preserve contrast over the actual video background.
- Highlight bars, feathered gradient masks, and Glow effects must preserve crisp
  letter edges instead of making premium text look soft.
- Background cards and object-animation palettes should preserve text/object readability before style.
- Dimming and desaturation for B-roll text must not destroy proof color or action detail.
- Brand/client colors should stay consistent across AE backgrounds, icons, text highlights, glows, and overlays.
- Texture effects such as grid, noise, vignette, glow, and turbulent displacement should add depth without reducing contrast.
- Warm glow, grain, radial blur, and vignette should support hope or memory without hiding faces, proof, or captions.
- Editorial paper palettes should preserve contrast for map labels, chart values, source text, route lines, and highlights.
- Text color, gradient, blend mode, shadow, and stroke must preserve readable edges on the actual background.
- Drop shadow is functional when it improves readability; heavy default shadow is a mistake when contrast already works.
- Glow, flicker, fast background slices, and distortion tints should avoid banding, muddy silhouettes, and compression dirt.
- Transparent overlay edges should be reviewed over bright and dark backgrounds
  to catch black/white halos, premultiply issues, and glow contamination.
- Low-key firelight composites should darken and warm the base only enough to
  motivate the light source while preserving faces, captions, and scene detail.
- Green-screen VFX and exported foreground cutouts should be color-matched enough
  that layer boundaries do not reveal the trick.
- Eye/power overlays should be matched for brightness, glow, and contrast so they
  do not erase expression or create compression dirt.
- Archival film frames, particles, fade, glass overlays, and vignette should
  remain subordinate to readable source text, faces, dates, and captions.

## Common Mistakes

- Adding graphics because they are part of a channel habit, not because the scene needs them.
- Making maps or route graphics disappear before the viewer can understand them.
- Using a match cut that implies a false relationship.
- Prioritizing lower thirds over subtitles.
- Letting motion style fight the subject tone.
- Using letterbox because it looks cinematic rather than because it means something.
- Breaking a treatment grammar without a perspective or story reason.
- Rotating, zooming, or fast-cutting evidence so viewers cannot inspect it.
- Forgetting hold keyframes after a zoom or pan.
- Adding arrows or overlays that repeat the obvious instead of clarifying focus.
- Stacking title text, card text, captions, and arrows until nothing reads cleanly.
- Rendering final title/caption typography with Pillow/PIL instead of
  HyperFrames, Remotion, or ASS.
- Adding title/motion graphics to a final render without user-approved approval
  frames.
- Suppressing captions under title graphics without explicit user approval.
- Showing every spoken word as text instead of visualizing the idea.
- Using stock footage that matches a keyword but not the actual meaning.
- Letting the subject drift away from center during a talking-head tracking crop.
- Leaving visual-stack elements onscreen after the concept has passed.
- Saving a Fusion effect to Power Bins before checking it at phone size.
- Letting gradient, glow, or glitch text become less readable than plain captions.
- Using white flashes so often that they become tiring or hide the cut.
- Using full-screen object animation where a simple B-roll or text card would be clearer.
- Darkening B-roll until the text reads but the proof disappears.
- Letting copied adjustment zooms affect unintended sections.
- Starting AE animation before the segment visual plan is clear.
- Applying a zoom to original footage but not the matching rotoscope or cutout duplicate.
- Letting dense AE layers vanish without an out animation or motivated overlay transition.
- Saving presets before checking them at phone size with captions and proof visible.
- Building complex animation without a sketch.
- Exporting simple-tool animations too low-resolution for later zooms.
- Using text pop or texture overlays so often that they become visual noise.
- Using viral text effects on every line until the edit loses hierarchy.
- Adding premium text masks, gradients, or glow before the words have a clear
  hierarchy, phrase break, and contrast plan.
- Hard-coding Remotion typography effects to global timeline frames instead of
  local Sequence timing.
- Letting a generated FFmpeg filter graph become too long to inspect or
  reproduce from structured data.
- Using non-deterministic random values for glitch, scramble, or jitter text.
- Exporting transparent Remotion or FFmpeg overlays through a codec/pixel format
  that discards alpha.
- Accepting a black box behind an overlay instead of checking alpha, codec,
  pixel format, and import interpretation.
- Using `xfade` directly where an in-place alpha reveal is needed.
- Adding new motion to a long human section only because the timeline feels visually quiet.
- Making the caption or texture treatment more noticeable than the human reaction.
- Copying another publisher's logo or exact graphic identity.
- Letting paper texture, FPS lag, or grid overlays make data/source text unreadable.
- Building charts without values/source notes.
- Using route arrows or markers that hide the map labels they explain.
- Animating typography before deciding the impact word, line break, and font role.
- Placing live-action text over the emotional subject, product, lamp, UI, or evidence instead of available negative space.
- Using gradients, shadows, or composite modes that make the type slower to read than plain text.
- Using Fusion for a basic keyframe move that the Edit page inspector can handle.
- Trusting a tracked graphic or Magic Mask matte without reviewing the full affected range.
- Spending heavy motion effort on an intro that does not improve brand trust or viewer outcome.
- Letting flicker, glow, or 3D camera movement make the brand or proof harder to read.
- Using keyframes because they are available rather than because the path or focus needs custom control.
- Changing multiple properties when the motion job only needed one property
  keyframed.
- Rotating or zooming without checking corners, captions, and proof at delivery size.
- Leaving a sticker, cutout, or route marker onscreen after its concept has passed.
- Applying glow to the whole frame when only the subject detail needs emphasis.
- Extending an effect tail until the frame becomes muddy or the next beat is delayed.
- Pre-rendering dense effects without preserving source layers or transition handles.
- Baking eye/power overlays before checking drift, duration, and beat alignment.
- Adding multiple Pendulum passes before checking the combined transition readability.
- Adding transition shake before the actual turbulent/wide-angle flow can be judged.
- Using graph presets as polish without checking the actual start, acceleration, settle, and focal safety.
- Adding motion blur to hide rough keyframe timing instead of fixing the curve.
- Scaling to extreme values or stacking zoom exports when the image becomes too soft.
- Choosing a stock fire, sky, smoke, or atmosphere overlay because the keyword
  matches while perspective, light, or motion direction does not.
- Using global glow or warmth to hide visible layer mismatch instead of fixing
  overlay placement, mask edges, and color matching.
- Treating mobile auto tools as final: Remove Background, Tracking, Chroma Key,
  and Split masks all need full-range playback review.
- Adding pro effects after filming without negative space, locked camera
  settings, or rights-safe assets.
- Using blur, texture, film frames, or parallax as documentary style while source
  evidence becomes harder to read.
- Leaving both switch-focus subjects blurry during the handoff.
- Using generated or downloaded cutouts as if they were documentary evidence
  without labeling or source rights.

## QC Checklist

- Text and graphics do not overlap key video content.
- The viewer has enough time to parse each graphic.
- Motion starts and ends cleanly without stray frames.
- Graphic style matches subject tone.
- Match cuts have explicit meaning and no harmful unintended implication.
- Maps/routes are readable before they disappear.
- Reality/fantasy treatment has clear meaning and consistent application.
- Treatment exceptions are intentional and tied to perspective, belief, or proof.
- Rotation, zoom, or aspect changes do not hide evidence, faces, maps, or body language.
- Focus zooms hold steady after landing and do not drift away from the target.
- Overlays clear before they compete with the next beat.
- Text, captions, and arrows remain readable at phone size.
- Each graphic has a named purpose and one primary focus.
- Visual stack hierarchy is clear between text, icons, stock footage, captions, and background.
- Speaker tracking preserves face, hands, proof, and caption safety.
- Highlight color or glow improves meaning without reducing readability.
- Fusion animations use intentional Spline easing and preserve vertical safe areas.
- Text+ premium captions are readable and do not duplicate auto captions unintentionally.
- White flashes are brief, intentional, and cleanly rendered.
- Filler cards, B-roll text, and full-screen animations are chosen by line importance.
- B-roll text remains readable while preserving the underlying proof or action.
- Full-screen object animations settle before the viewer needs to understand the line.
- Adjustment-layer zooms keep captions, faces, UI, products, and proof safe.
- Every AE segment has a written visual job before animation begins.
- Speaker eyes stay stable through hook zooms.
- Track-matte textures keep the original subject recognizable.
- Camera/null moves do not reveal empty edges.
- Captions remain visible, repositioned, or have a documented user-approved
  suppression exception when animated text already carries the line.
- Animations have sketches or written plans before build.
- Texture overlays have named jobs and preserve readability.
- Text pop presets settle before the phrase needs to be read.
- Slow human motion choices preserve faces, proof artifacts, captions, and warm color readability.
- Any first-seconds dynamic zoom has a clear focus target and does not crop the hook.
- Editorial maps, charts, documents, and screenshot focus sequences remain readable after texture, motion, and low-FPS effects.
- Graphic assets have rights/source notes and do not imply false endorsement.
- Typography has one clear primary word or phrase per scene.
- Text line breaks, spacing, and placement remain readable after motion and texture are applied.
- Viral text effects resolve to readable text, verified final numbers, or clean
  cutout/chroma edges before the next beat.
- Highlight wipes, gradient type, Player 3 caption compounds, staggered blur
  reveals, and glow sweeps preserve readable text at phone size.
- Programmatic typography renders have logged filter/component metadata,
  available fonts, deterministic seeds, and contact sheets around reveal states.
- HyperFrames or Remotion generated final title cards, hook cards, chapter cards,
  stat cards, lower thirds, and major motion-typography overlays.
- Approval frames/contact sheets were reviewed by the user before final
  compositing.
- Final captions are ASS captions and were not replaced by the motion graphic.
- Transparent overlays have verified alpha, transparent empty areas, and clean
  edges over bright and dark backgrounds.
- FFmpeg alpha masks have correct polarity and matching foreground/mask stream
  properties before `alphamerge`.
- Remotion text masks/glitches behave consistently across preview and final
  render.
- FFmpeg drawtext overlays use readable enable windows and avoid brittle
  unreviewed word chains.
- Live-action text sits in negative space or tracks cleanly without covering the focal point.
- Resolve Fusion composites have intentional node order, masks/tracks remain
  stable, and the timeline view preserves captions, faces, proof, and action.
- Branded intros show brand, credible proof, and outcome with one primary visual focus at a time.
- Intro text flicker, motion blur, 3D movement, glow, and underline effects remain readable at delivery size.
- The finished intro is trimmed to platform duration instead of padded with unused build time.
- CapCut-style keyframed paths hold long enough to read and clear before competing with the next focus.
- Compound-wrapper animations keep the source sticker/text editable and do not
  duplicate the source layer's own in/out animation.
- Tilt, zoom, and tracking keyframes preserve captions, faces, proof, UI, products, and frame edges.
- Cutout/object animation fits the project tone and asset rights are ready for real projects.
- Character-mask glow/shadow has clean edges and a named emphasis target.
- Turbulent/wide-angle transition motion preserves action readability and caption safety.
- Eye/power overlays stay aligned and readable through playback.
- Pendulum movement has a named energy job and does not hide the subject or next pose.
- Effect-tail extensions clear before the next focus and avoid muddy duplicate layers.
- Heavy overlay pre-renders retain editable source layers or revision paths.
- Graph-shaped keyframes have intentional start/end speed and do not feel accidentally linear.
- Motion blur preserves faces, captions, proof, UI, products, and action.
- Stacked zoom passes are justified by the style and do not destroy source detail.
- Still-image overlays align with plausible sources, mask edges are invisible,
  and the composite remains readable at delivery size.
- Local flicker remains weaker than or consistent with its light source and does
  not cover captions, faces, proof, UI, products, or key action.
- Subject-sandwich, chroma-key, tracking, PiP, split-mask, clone, and green-screen
  effects preserve captions, faces, proof, UI, products, and action at delivery
  size.
- Switch-focus, research overlay, archival, filmed-screen, and parallax effects
  preserve one primary focus, readable evidence, captions, and asset rights.
