# UGC Ad Editing

## Purpose

Guide direct-response UGC ads, product demos, testimonials, proof, objections,
typography, and CTA pacing.

## When To Use This Skill

- Editing creator-style ads for social platforms.
- Turning raw testimonials or demos into persuasive short ads.
- Choosing readable claim, proof, offer, or CTA text for performance-led content.
- Matching a client reference while keeping the ad easy to understand.

## Core Principles

- Open with the problem, result, objection, or curiosity gap.
- Show product proof early.
- Cut filler but keep authentic human texture.
- Make the CTA clear without letting it feel detached from the story.
- Performance typography should make the product, result, offer, or CTA readable
  before decorative style is noticed.
- Client references define the style lane, but readability and claim clarity still
  decide the final text treatment.

## Timing Rules

- The main product/result claim should be readable in the opening seconds when it
  is part of the hook.
- CTA text should hold long enough to read after the viewer has seen proof or the
  offer.
- Text animation should not delay the claim, objection, proof, or CTA.

## Motion Rules

- Use restrained motion for ad text unless the brand/reference demands high energy.
- Place claim, proof, and CTA text where they do not cover product use, face,
  demonstration, or social proof.

## Sound Rules

- SFX can mark proof or CTA reveals only when speech and product clarity remain
  dominant.

## Caption Rules

- Captions, offer text, proof labels, and CTA should have separate roles and safe
  screen regions.
- Remove duplicate captions when claim or CTA text already carries the words,
  unless accessibility requires both.

## Color Rules

- Brand colors are useful only if the product/result claim remains readable.
- Avoid low-contrast premium styling on claims, price, offer, or CTA text.
- Drop shadow, stroke, gradient, or glow must improve readability, not just style.

## Tool Implementation Notes

- For Remotion, model ad text by role: claim, proof, objection, offer, CTA, and
  legal/disclaimer.
- For ffmpeg, render claim, proof, and CTA text as separate timed layers and review
  phone-size contact sheets.
- For CapCut/Premiere, simplify fancy templates when they slow down the claim or
  CTA.

## Common Mistakes

- Product claims are exaggerated by the edit.
- Typography looks artistic but the viewer cannot read the offer quickly.
- CTA text competes with captions or product proof.
- A client reference is copied without checking whether the ad still communicates.
- Captions, graphics, and CTA overwhelm authenticity.

## QC Checklist

- Product claims are not exaggerated by the edit.
- The viewer sees the product or result clearly.
- Product/result/offer/CTA text is readable at phone size.
- Captions, proof labels, offer text, and CTA do not collide.
- The type style matches the client reference without sacrificing comprehension.
- A cleaner readability-first variant is considered when the ad is performance-led.
