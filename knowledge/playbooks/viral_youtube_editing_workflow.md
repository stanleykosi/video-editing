# Viral YouTube Editing Workflow

## Purpose

Guide full viral-style YouTube editing workflows from audience and idea fit through
script structure, asset planning, Premiere assembly, animation, SFX, color, QC,
and publish threshold.

## When To Use This Skill

- Editing a scripted YouTube video, creator essay, tutorial, or high-effort viral-style explainer.
- Deciding whether the idea and script are strong enough before polish begins.
- Mapping a script into assets, animations, text, music, SFX, overlays, and presets.
- Building a fancy edit while keeping effects subordinate to the message.
- Balancing continuous improvement with a realistic publish threshold.
- Deciding whether a YouTube opener should skip a legacy bumper or use a premium show/brand intro.

## Core Principles

- Editing cannot rescue a boring idea or unclear script; fix the audience promise first.
- Match editing style to the audience and creator voice instead of copying another niche.
- Add a secondary story layer only when it deepens the main video without delaying the promise too much.
- Color-code or tag script beats before editing so asset gathering becomes deliberate.
- Build voiceover flow first, then decide whether early music helps establish vibe and beat timing.
- Sketch animations before building them; choose the simplest tool that can communicate the idea.
- Text has jobs: captions for comprehension, center/emphasis text for important ideas, and scene-attached text for world integration.
- Effects should complement what is being said; simple choices often beat maximal effects.
- Sound design is a major polish layer, but every SFX cue needs a visible or story job.
- Learn one new technique per project, then publish when core QC passes instead of chasing endless perfection.
- Ordinary 8-12 minute YouTube videos usually need immediate value, not an old channel logo bumper.
- A formal intro is justified only when show identity, external proof, authority, or outcome packaging makes the video feel more trustworthy faster.

## Timing Rules

- Fix idea, audience, script, and structure before heavy timeline polish.
- Cut awkward pauses and dragged lines, but preserve meaningful space when the pause makes the story worth waiting for.
- Music-first assembly is valid when vibe matters, but the voiceover must remain clear before and after beat cuts.
- Text pop timing can use 70% scale, 110% overshoot around 7 frames later, and 100% settle about 5 frames after that.
- Animation reveals should follow the voiceover's explanation order, not the editor's tool experimentation.
- Publish only after blockers are cleared; move nice-to-have improvements into the next-project backlog.
- For ordinary mid-length YouTube, start delivering the title promise before any formal bumper.
- For podcast/show/course-style YouTube packaging, keep the formal intro around 10-15 seconds maximum and hand off cleanly to the body.

## Motion Rules

- Keep focal points stable across zooms and cuts using guides, center framing, eyeline, or rule-of-thirds targets.
- Use Transform scale/position curves instead of linear motion for zooms and text movement.
- Use simple design-tool animations for simple ideas and After Effects/Remotion for complex path, typewriter, glow, or diagram work.
- Use overlays with Screen/Add-style blending only when they highlight, transition, or add a deliberate texture.
- Save presets only after checking that they work across different text lengths, assets, and sections.
- Premium intro text, logo, flicker, and 3D movement should guide brand -> proof -> outcome, not show off animation.

## Sound Rules

- Early music can define vibe, but it must be licensed/platform-safe or marked temp-only.
- Beat cuts should land on meaningful visual or story moments, not every beat.
- Do an isolated SFX spotting pass when useful, then restore dialogue/music and lower SFX until speech stays dominant.
- Start with whoosh, riser, and impact families, then add context sounds only when they fit the visual beat.
- Intro hits and drum cues should be locked to visible events and duck or fade before proof quotes or body speech.

## Caption Rules

- Captions should not appear on every beat if the constant text becomes irritating or competes with visual text.
- Use center/emphasis text for important ideas that captions cannot carry cleanly.
- Scene-attached text should look anchored to the screen, wall, object, or background it belongs to.
- Captions and emphasis text should not duplicate each other unless accessibility requires it.
- During designed intro text, disable ordinary captions unless they add accessibility without clutter.

## Color Rules

- Apply basic Lumetri or correction late enough that the rough edit is stable.
- Correct exposure, contrast, saturation, and shot mismatch while preserving faces, proof, and text readability.
- Effects, overlays, and glows should fit the chosen style without creating muddy contrast or compression dirt.

## Tool Implementation Notes

- Premiere: build voiceover first, add music early only when useful, layer assets from the script color map, use Transform/text/overlay presets, then do SFX, color, and export QC.
- After Effects: use sketches to build text, shapes, pen paths, typewriter, Trim Paths, glow, and complex animation systems.
- Remotion: model the workflow with audience, scriptBeats, colorTags, assets, musicCue, visualComponents, sfxEvents, and publishDecision data.
- ffmpeg: use rough exports, contact sheets, loudness checks, overlay/caption probes, and final decode validation.
- CapCut/Canva: build simple animations and text/shape graphics quickly, export sharp enough for timeline zooms, then check readability.

## Common Mistakes

- Copying a style that does not fit the target audience.
- Starting with effects before idea, script, and structure pass.
- Editing with no script asset map, then hunting for visuals mid-timeline.
- Letting music energy hide weak pacing.
- Building complex animations without sketches.
- Using pop text or overlays constantly until they become noise.
- Doing SFX in isolation and forgetting to re-check the full mix.
- Treating every possible improvement as a reason not to publish.
- Keeping a legacy bumper because it feels professional even though it delays the title promise.
- Letting intro motion and SFX overpower the trust proof or viewer outcome.

## QC Checklist

- Target audience, idea promise, and style lane are written.
- Script has a clear structure and any secondary story layer supports the main promise.
- Script beats are tagged with asset/visual needs before the heavy edit.
- Voiceover is trimmed for flow before music drives beat cuts.
- Music is licensed/platform-safe or marked temp-only.
- Visual assets and animations map to script beats.
- Text, overlays, captions, and zooms have one primary focus at each moment.
- SFX cues match visible motion or story beats and stay below dialogue.
- Basic color correction preserves faces, proof, and text readability.
- Remaining improvements are optional; blocking QC issues are cleared before publish.
- Any formal intro has a named brand/proof/outcome purpose and meets the correct duration cap.
- If the intro is removed, the video loses trust/package value rather than simply becoming faster.
