# Beat Sync

## Purpose

Guide music-aware cutting, rhythmic emphasis, needle-drop cue timing, beat
mapping, and synchronized motion.

## When To Use This Skill

- Editing montages, anime edits, sports highlights, ads, or music-driven shorts.
- Syncing cuts, speed ramps, impacts, captions, or motion accents to a track.
- Using a strong music cue or needle drop as a section shift or retention moment.
- Timing licensed music cues to action, reveals, product-placement beats, or emotional turns.
- Bridging a track from diegetic source sound into full-score energy.
- Aligning whooshes, hits, shakes, punch-ins, and overlay accents to visible motion.
- Building anticipation with reverse hits before short-form questions, reveals, or transitions.
- Adding music early under voiceover to establish vibe and selective beat-aware visual cuts.
- Building contrast-based intensity drops where sound and cut density rise, then release into calm.
- Shortening music in Resolve/Fairlight or any NLE while preserving beat, phrase,
  or waveform continuity.
- Syncing premium intro logo, text, flicker, underline, and camera accents to music pulses and SFX hits.
- Speed-matching CapCut/action/AMV clips so a pose, impact, reveal, or transformation lands on a beat.

## Core Principles

- Map the song structure before locking the edit.
- Put major visual changes on strong beats unless an off-beat choice is intentional.
- Use micro-sync for accents and macro-sync for section changes.
- Avoid forcing every cut to a beat if the story needs air.
- Do not choose a track only because it is recognizable; it must fit the story and be licensed.
- Major music cues should land on story turns, reveals, payoffs, action changes, or section resets.
- Plan the exit from a music-driven moment so the next story beat is not swallowed.
- Music timing should support story clarity before it shows off editing precision.
- Music tempo should fit the voice, scene pace, and video tone; it should not fight a slow or unclear cut.
- Motion-synced SFX should land on real movement, text pops, icon pops, or transition impacts.
- Voiceover flow comes first; beat cuts should support script logic rather than override it.
- A contrast drop does not need a musical downbeat if the sound build, cut density, and story reset make the release clear.
- Manual music edits should use beats, phrase endings, or matching waveform
  shapes; crossfade the join when the splice is audible.
- In premium intros, micro-sync can be stricter than ordinary motion accents: the hit should feel married to the exact visible frame.
- Speed matching is a per-clip decision: mark the beat and action peak, then adjust playback speed until they meet without losing readability.
- If a visual effect needs a slowed alignment window, the transformed frame still has to land on the beat after the overlay pass.

## Timing Rules

- In high-energy sections, place the main action cut or motion accent within 1-2 frames of the intended beat.
- Align the strongest cue, pickup, downbeat, or drop to a meaningful visual turn.
- Use a pre-cue only long enough to build anticipation; trim it if it delays the payoff.
- Escalate the track when the visual action escalates, or deliberately drop out for contrast.
- Duck, filter, or exit music before story-critical dialogue returns.
- SFX hits, shakes, and punch zooms should land within 1-2 frames of the intended visual impact when sync matters.
- If music only makes a slow section feel less slow, tighten the section before locking the cue.
- Reverse hits should resolve into a real question, reveal, transition object, or payoff beat.
- Text/icon pop SFX should land within 1-2 frames of the pop or motion start.
- For voiceover-led videos, add beat markers only after the VO is trimmed enough that music will not hide pacing problems.
- For short-form contrast drops, keep the black/silence reset under one second unless the pause is intentionally cinematic.
- When shortening a music bed manually, cut on beat, phrase end, or similar
  waveform peak and listen through the splice before locking it.
- Premium intro hits should land within 0-1 frames of logo, text, flicker, underline, camera, cut, or transition events.
- Around 120 BPM can be a useful starting pulse for fast intro packaging, but the brand and speech pace decide the final tempo.
- Add visible beat markers before speed-matching a run of action clips.
- Speed-adjusted action peaks should land within 1-2 frames of the target beat when high-energy sync matters.
- Eye or power-overlay reveals should be checked after speed adjustment because small-detail alignment can shift perceived timing.
- Pendulum peaks should land on the intended beat or transition peak only when the movement still leaves the next pose readable.
- Preview the previous clip, synced clip, and next cut before locking a speed change.

## Motion Rules

- Sync speed ramps, punch zooms, wide-shot reveals, or impact cuts only where they clarify the action or story turn.
- Do not add motion accents just because the track has energy.
- If an off-beat motion choice is used, the off-beat should create tension, comedy, or discomfort on purpose.
- Repeated motion accents need SFX variation so beat sync does not feel mechanically pasted.
- Statement zooms should receive accents only when the line is important enough to deserve emphasis.
- Script-driven visuals should not be moved to a beat if doing so makes the spoken idea late or confusing.
- Speed ramps or speed matches should preserve the readable pose, motion direction, and transition handles.
- Speed adjustments made for overlay alignment must also preserve the musical beat, not only the mask or overlay fit.

## Sound Rules

- Use beat, cue, drop, escalation, duck, and exit markers before finalizing music-led sections.
- Keep dialogue intelligible under music-driven edits.
- For diegetic bridges, begin with a plausible source-filtered sound and open into the full mix on a story beat.
- Treat unlicensed tracks as temp references until cleared.
- Music should match the pacing of the voice and visual rhythm; avoid using energy to hide unclear structure.
- SFX should land on real starts, stops, impacts, or reveals, not just near them.
- Keep whooshes and hits low enough under dialogue that sync does not damage comprehension.
- Early music is a timing guide, not a permanent lock, until license and final mix are approved.
- Contrast builds can use risers, whooshes, ambience, or tighter cuts, but the release must re-enter dialogue cleanly.
- AI music retiming can be a useful draft where available, but the final check is
  whether the shortened track still feels musical under the scene.
- Intro beds should fade, duck, or resolve before proof quotes or body speech become hard to understand.

## Caption Rules

- Captions can emphasize lyrics or dialogue only when they do not compete with the music-led visual payoff.
- Do not cover the action, reveal, or emotional turn receiving the cue.

## Color Rules

- No direct color rule; preserve action readability during music-led ramps, flashes, or stylized sections.

## Tool Implementation Notes

- For ffmpeg, use waveform review, frame-accurate trims, `volume`, `afade`, and EQ/filter automation around cue points.
- For Remotion, store `cueFrame`, `dropFrame`, `escalationFrame`, `duckFrames`, and `exitFrame` as explicit timeline data.
- For Blender, use audio markers to sync camera/object animation to the selected cue.
- For CapCut, use beat markers and manual trims rather than relying only on auto-beat cuts.
- For CapCut speed matching, use the Speed control after beat markers are placed, adjust the slider per clip, and re-preview adjacent clips before adding transitions.
- For Premiere, add markers for song cue, downbeat, visual turn, escalation, duck, and exit; slip audio or picture until the cue lands.
- In Resolve/Fairlight, align music to the story beat first, then trim or splice
  with beat/waveform review and fades/crossfades where needed.

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the strongest cue on a random shot.
- Cutting every shot to the beat after the main payoff.
- Letting track tempo fight the action pace.
- Forgetting to duck before dialogue.
- Using a fun cue that is tonally wrong for the scene.
- Animating to a temp track before licensing or final track choice is confirmed.
- Repeating the same whoosh or hit on every beat.
- Letting a high-energy track overpower the pace and tone of the speaker.
- Using reverse hits before nothing meaningful happens.
- Syncing SFX to every small movement instead of the movements that matter.
- Cutting script visuals to music beats when the voiceover needs different timing.
- Adding a drop gap with no preceding build or story reset.
- Accepting an AI-retimed or manually spliced music edit without listening for a bump.
- Following the intro beat grid when it makes brand, proof, or outcome text appear too late to read.
- Reusing one speed value across multiple action clips instead of matching each action peak to the beat.
- Slowing a clip for an overlay and forgetting to re-check the transformed beat against the music.
- Adding transition shake or SFX before the speed-matched visual hit is locked.

## QC Checklist

- Important cuts land close enough to the intended beat to feel deliberate.
- The strongest music moments correspond to meaningful visual or story moments.
- Audio transitions are smooth at section changes.
- Beat sync does not damage story clarity.
- Music cues are licensed, platform-safe, or marked temp-only.
- Dialogue is clear immediately before and after cue-heavy moments.
- The edit avoids unnecessary beat-cutting after the payoff lands.
- Repeated SFX and motion accents vary enough to feel intentional.
- Music energy matches the spoken pace and visual rhythm.
- Reverse hits resolve into real reveals, questions, or transitions.
- Text pop, icon pop, and zoom accents land within intentional sync tolerance.
- Beat cuts preserve voiceover comprehension and story order.
- Contrast drops follow a clear build and do not sound like accidental audio dropouts.
- Shortened music beds preserve beat/phrase continuity and do not distract from dialogue.
- Intro logo, text, underline, flicker, and transition hits land on visible events, not only near them.
- Intro music supports the handoff to speech instead of swallowing the first body line.
- Speed-matched action clips land on beat while remaining readable at delivery size.
- Small-detail transformation overlays land on beat after speed adjustment and pre-render replacement.
- Beat markers, action peaks, and transition handles are present before final transition polish.
