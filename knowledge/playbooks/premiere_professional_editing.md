# Premiere Professional Editing

## Purpose

Preserve professional NLE editing practices and map them to agent workflows across
Premiere, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut, ffmpeg, and Remotion.

## When To Use This Skill

- Planning or executing timeline organization, rough cuts, polish passes, audio cleanup, or delivery.
- Translating NLE tutorials into repeatable agent actions.
- Editing talking-head, tutorial, commentary, documentary, or YouTube videos.
- Matching professional handoff expectations with clear bins, tracks, sequences, markers, and exports.
- Deciding when to use J-cuts, L-cuts, punch-ins, overlays, nested/compound clips, or export checks.
- Building Premiere-style short-form edits with rulers/guides, subject tracking, Essential Graphics text, masks, caption styles, and SFX markers.
- Building Premiere Reel workflows with silence removal, J-cuts, one-word captions, hook text design, body visual stacks, adjustment-layer zooms, and music auditioning.
- Building DaVinci Resolve vertical shorts with Edit page assembly, Fusion motion, Text+, Power Bins, and shortcut-driven workflow.
- Preparing a Premiere timeline for an After Effects animation pass and finishing the rendered animation with overlays, SFX, music, captions, and export QC.
- Building scripted viral-style YouTube edits from voiceover, script tags, music, assets, text, overlays, SFX, color, and final publish QC.
- Translating full DaVinci Resolve workflows into general NLE phases: media,
  assembly, effects, color, audio mix, and delivery.

## Core Principles

- Keep source media untouched.
- Separate raw media, selects, rough cuts, polish sequences, previews, finals, and exports.
- Build the rough cut before investing in captions, effects, SFX, or color polish.
- Use markers, bins, labels, track names, and duplicate sequences as cognitive tools.
- Prefer reversible edits and reproducible render scripts.
- Keep dialogue, music, ambience, and SFX on separate tracks or logical groups.
- Save before risky timeline operations and keep a clear version trail.
- Use playback-resolution/proxy choices to make editing responsive without changing final export quality.
- Export only from the intended range, sequence, and delivery settings.
- Use rulers/guides and Effect Controls when manually tracking a talking-head subject in vertical or tight crops.
- Treat auto-transcription and caption creation as a draft until chunks, style, placement, and duplicate text are checked.
- Do not upgrade captions to graphics until transcript text, timing, chunking, and lower-third placement are approved.
- Use adjustment layers for reusable section polish, but keep their ranges explicit and easy to disable.
- In Resolve, use vertical project settings before judging short-form caption or overlay placement.
- Use Power Bins or equivalent preset libraries for effects that have passed QC and should recur across projects.
- For Premiere plus After Effects Reels, lock a stable prepared cut before animation, then finish audio/captions after the AE render is aligned.
- A reusable project template should separate raw footage, prepared renders, AE renders, overlays, SFX, music, captions, and exports.
- For scripted workflows, map script beats to asset types before importing and layering visuals.
- Early music can be useful after voiceover trimming, but keep it replaceable until license and final mix checks pass.
- Resolve's page model maps cleanly to pro NLE passes: organize media, build
  story, add effects/graphics, match color, finish audio, then export from the
  intended range and profile.

## Timing Rules

- Remove accidental opening silence or dead air before the first useful line or visual.
- For short-form or high-retention talking-head edits, remove dead air over 300 ms unless it has a clear job.
- Use J/L cuts and short audio crossfades when hard audio/video cuts feel harsh.
- Add punch-ins or motivated visuals only after the speech timing is readable.
- Keep rough-cut timing separate from polish timing; do not lock motion before the spoken structure is stable.
- Export a short preview of the opening, dense graphics, and major audio transitions before final render.
- For dense shorts, mark hook, rest beat, statement zooms, visual stack sections, SFX impacts, and caption review points.
- Resolve white flashes should peak around the cut and fade down within a few frames.
- Premiere short-form caption animation should be applied only after generated caption chunks are corrected.
- One-word social captions can start around 7 characters, 2 seconds max duration, and single-line layout, then be manually reviewed.
- Adjustment-layer zoom-blur polish should settle before the viewer needs to read captions, text, UI, or proof.
- Export a prepared 1080x1920 render for AE only after sync, trimming, rough smoothing, and basic correction are checked.
- Final captions, SFX, and music should be placed against the visible AE render, not an earlier rough-cut timing.
- Voiceover should be trimmed for flow before music or beat cuts shape the visual pass.
- Text pop timing can start at 70% scale, overshoot to 110% about 7 frames later, and settle to 100% about 5 frames after that.
- In Resolve or Premiere-style editing, use source In/Out ranges before placing
  footage and remove accidental leading black, gaps, and empty audio holes early.

## Motion Rules

- Use scale/position keyframes to guide focus, hide cuts, or clarify the current spoken point.
- Add hold keyframes when the viewer needs to read the zoomed-in target.
- Use overlays, arrows, text, and background dimming only when they reduce search time.
- Do not stack multiple attention devices when one crop, arrow, or overlay is enough.
- Preview motion and text at delivery size, especially vertical shorts.
- Use position and scale keyframes to keep the speaker centered during movement.
- Use masks, feather, nested sequences, and Essential Graphics for visual stacks only after the rough cut is stable.
- In Resolve, use Fusion Transform nodes and Spline easing for reusable zooms and Text+ motion.
- Check adjustment clips so they only affect the intended range.
- For hook text design, stack only the needed hook words, emphasize one keyword, and avoid duplicating ordinary captions.
- For B-roll text sequences, put the readability adjustment between text and footage so text reads without destroying proof detail.
- For full-screen object animations, build from background, main object, support shapes, and hierarchy text.
- Use a prepared render for AE roundtrips so animation work is based on a stable vertical frame.
- Align AE renders to the original timeline, then remove duplicate render audio unless it is intentionally used.
- Use guides, center framing, eyeline, or rule-of-thirds targets so focus stays stable across zooms and cuts.
- Use Screen/Add blend modes for black-background texture overlays and trim them to specific highlight or transition beats.
- Use an anchor wide shot and cut on action when closeups or inserts must improve
  a sequence without breaking spatial clarity.

## Sound Rules

- Treat speech as the anchor unless the section is intentionally music-led.
- Duck music before story-critical words begin.
- Use volume keyframes for local repairs instead of raising entire clips by default.
- Keep SFX on separate tracks and fade edges to avoid clicks.
- Rotate repeated whooshes or hits so the edit does not sound copied and pasted.
- Check the final mix for red/clipped peaks, not just individual clips.
- Use markers for whoosh, hit, reverse hit, and transition impact frames.
- Mute or delete unintended B-roll source audio in short-form A-roll edits.
- Resolve in-app voiceover recordings still need level, noise, and timing checks.
- Audition short-form music under the finished edit and document license/platform status before locking the version.
- Add whooshes, typewriter sounds, camera cues, and reward hits only after the AE animation render is visible in Premiere.
- For isolated SFX spotting, temporarily mute dialogue/music only long enough to place cues, then restore the full mix and rebalance.
- In Fairlight or Premiere equivalents, keep dialogue, ambience, music, and SFX
  on controllable tracks or buses/submixes before final export.

## Caption Rules

- Caption timing follows the audible speech, including J/L cut overlaps.
- Captions do not cover faces, UI, products, proof visuals, or the action being discussed.
- Lower thirds and overlays must move or disappear when they conflict with subtitles.
- AI-generated captions need manual review for wording, emphasis, line breaks, and filler clutter.
- Short-form captions can use one- or two-word chunks, but only after checking readability and duplicate visual text.
- Upgrade captions to graphics only after transcript timing and wording are correct.
- Resolve simple captions can come from AI subtitle tools; premium emphasis should use Text+ only on key moments.
- Text+ stacks should not duplicate auto captions unless the duplicate is intentional emphasis.
- Premiere one-word captions should remain in a lower-third safe area and move when they collide with hook text, B-roll text, or full-screen hierarchy text.
- Caption graphics should be reviewed after presets are applied, not only before conversion.
- When captions are created after an AE pass, disable or opacity-fade captions over dense animation that already carries the same words.
- Use center/emphasis text when captions are not enough, but avoid text popping on every sentence.

## Color Rules

- Preserve faces, UI, evidence, and proof detail before applying stylized looks.
- Keep color polish late enough that timing changes do not churn rendered effects.
- Check that overlays and captions remain readable over the final grade.

## Tool Implementation Notes

- Premiere: use duplicate sequences, bins, labels, markers, linked selection toggles, Effect Controls, Essential Graphics, Essential Sound, manual keyframes, and Export range checks.
- Premiere captions: use Text > Transcribe Sequence and Create Captions as a draft, style one caption in Essential Graphics, apply the style, then upgrade to graphics only when animation/styling requires it.
- Premiere visual stacks: use nested sequences, masks with feather, glow or transform effects, stock layers, and guide overlays; keep layer names tied to spoken concepts.
- Premiere one-word captions: create captions from transcript with short single-line settings, style one caption, create a style, correct all chunks, then upgrade to graphics only for animation.
- Premiere hook text: duplicate or stack selected text layers, arrange for phone-size balance, emphasize one keyword with bold/color/glow, and keep duplicate captions visible, repositioned, or explicitly approved for suppression.
- Premiere B-roll text: use Source Monitor I/O points, drag video-only when needed, place text above B-roll, and use an adjustment layer for brightness/saturation readability treatment.
- Premiere adjustment zooms: apply Transform and Gaussian Blur to an adjustment layer, use scale 130 to 100 and blur 3 to 0 as a starting reveal, then duplicate only to selected sections.
- Premiere/AE roundtrip: sync external audio, remove unwanted source audio after sync, create the vertical prepared sequence, export the checked prepared render, animate in AE, import the render, align it, unlink/delete duplicate audio, then add overlays, SFX, music, captions, and final export checks.
- Premiere scripted viral workflow: tag script beats, import VO, trim awkward pauses, add licensed/platform-safe music early only if useful, layer tagged assets, build text/overlay presets, spot SFX, correct color, then run publish QC.
- DaVinci Resolve: use `Shift+9` for project settings, enable vertical resolution, assemble in Edit page, polish in Fusion, save reusable effects in Power Bins, and document shortcut mappings.
- DaVinci Resolve: use Media Pool organization, Edit page tracks, Inspector keyframes, linked selection, compound clips, Mixer/Fairlight controls, Deliver page range checks, and playback resolution for laggy systems.
- DaVinci Resolve full workflow: use project libraries and `.drp` backups,
  Media Pool bins/keywords/smart bins, waveform audio sync, Edit or Cut page
  assembly, Fusion nodes/tracking, Color scopes/stills/secondaries, Fairlight
  tracks/buses, and Deliver codec/range checks.
- CapCut: use split/extract-audio workflows, keyframes, text stroke/shadow, manual volume curves, and template review.
- ffmpeg: store EDL/concat decisions, use `trim`, `concat`, `atrim`, `acrossfade`, `volume`, `loudnorm`, `crop`, `scale`, `overlay`, and render short previews.
- Remotion: represent clips, overlays, captions, gain envelopes, focus boxes, and export variants as explicit data.

## Common Mistakes

- Polishing effects before the rough cut is understandable.
- Exporting the wrong in/out range or sequence version.
- Leaving missing media, missing fonts, black frames, or accidental overlays in the final render.
- Mixing all audio on one track and losing control over dialogue, music, and SFX.
- Using punch-ins that make the speaker's eyes jump unnaturally.
- Trusting auto-captions or auto-reframe without manual review.
- Forgetting that playback resolution/proxies affect preview speed, not final export quality.
- Upgrading captions to graphics before the transcript is corrected.
- Manual tracking without guides, causing subject drift.
- Letting Premiere preset stacks replace editorial judgment about focus and hierarchy.
- Saving untested Resolve effects into Power Bins.
- Leaving Fusion animation linear because Spline easing was not checked.
- Forgetting to remove B-roll audio under the speaker.
- Saving a caption or zoom preset before checking it across multiple short-form sections.
- Allowing an adjustment layer to affect unintended clips.
- Leaving duplicate captions under hook text, B-roll text, or full-screen hierarchy text.
- Exporting a prepared render with a muted required audio track.
- Starting AE animation from an uncorrected rough timeline that still has bad takes, harsh cuts, or wrong sequence settings.
- Forgetting to remove duplicate AE render audio in the final Premiere sequence.
- Editing with no script asset map and wasting time searching for visuals mid-pass.
- Keeping early music after it stops fitting the final edit.
- Saving text, overlay, or SFX presets before checking them across real timeline cases.
- Choosing a delivery codec by habit instead of by web, client review, archive,
  alpha, or editing-handoff purpose.

## QC Checklist

- Source media remains untouched.
- Rough, polish, preview, final, and export versions are clearly separated.
- Timeline tracks are named or organized enough to repair dialogue, music, SFX, captions, and overlays.
- Dialogue, music, and SFX are controllable before final mix.
- Captions and overlays are checked at delivery size.
- Export range, resolution, frame rate, aspect ratio, and filename match the delivery target.
- Final render has no offline media, missing fonts, black frames, clipping, caption collisions, or accidental watermarks.
- Manual tracking keeps the speaker centered and caption-safe.
- Caption chunks, style, and graphic conversion are reviewed after transcription.
- SFX markers align with actual text pops, zooms, hits, or transitions.
- Resolve project is vertical before caption and overlay layout.
- Fusion effects use intentional easing and preserve crop/caption safety.
- Power Bin presets are named by job and checked at phone size before reuse.
- Premiere caption graphics were created only after caption correction.
- Premiere adjustment layers affect only intended section ranges.
- B-roll text treatments preserve proof detail while making text readable.
- Music candidates have tone-fit and license/platform notes.
- Prepared render is 1080x1920, synced, trimmed, corrected, and reviewed before AE work begins.
- AE render is watched before Premiere finishing.
- Dialogue, music, SFX, captions, and overlays are all unmuted/visible before final export.
- Script tags map to timeline assets and bins.
- Text pop presets are readable and do not collide with captions or proof.
- Isolated SFX pass has been followed by a full-mix review.
- Full Resolve-style exports have correct timeline/range, codec/container, audio,
  filename, and watched-output QC.
