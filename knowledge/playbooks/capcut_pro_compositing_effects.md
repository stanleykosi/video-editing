# CapCut Pro Compositing Effects

## Purpose

Build advanced CapCut-style composites that look planned rather than template
driven: subject-sandwich text, video-in-text portals, tracked stickers, slide-on
photo stacks, chapter cards, PiP borders, split-mask illusions, clone effects,
and green-screen VFX behind people.

## When To Use This Skill

- A CapCut edit needs pro-looking effects without leaving mobile or a simple NLE.
- Text, stickers, VFX, or photos need to interact with a person or scene rather
  than sit flat on top.
- A CapCut text effect needs subject-sandwich depth, video-in-text, outline
  reveal, chroma-key text, foreground-lift text, or editable compound text animation.
- The effect can be filmed or prepared with enough screen space, locked camera
  settings, clean assets, and rights-safe media.
- The agent needs ffmpeg, Remotion, Blender, CapCut, or Premiere equivalents for
  a CapCut tutorial trick.
- A character eye, symbol, or power detail needs a close-up overlay that lands
  on a beat without drifting.
- A documentary scene needs cutout subjects, title text, background texture, and
  foreground/background depth to interact rather than sit flat.

## Core Principles

- Plan the shot before the effect: leave negative space, keep targets visible,
  and lock camera settings for mask illusions.
- Layer order creates depth. Put the prepared base at the bottom, VFX or text in
  the middle, and a clean foreground cutout on top when something must sit behind
  the subject.
- Direct layer sandwiches can work without export/re-import when the duplicated
  foreground clip is frame-aligned and the top cutout edge is clean.
- Chroma key is a matte tool, not a magic polish pass. Choose separable colors,
  tune intensity carefully, and check edge fringe.
- Use built-in tracking only when the target stays visible; shorten or keyframe
  sections where tracking drifts.
- Export/re-import only when the effect needs a baked base; keep captions, final
  SFX, and revision-sensitive layers editable at the top level.
- For small-detail overlays, speed or slow the clip before compositing so the
  overlay can be aligned through playback rather than forced onto one paused frame.
- Treat app-library, downloaded, green-screen, sticker, music, and SFX assets as
  rights-managed assets before delivery.
- For documentary cutout composites, depth is useful only while faces, titles,
  source labels, captions, and rights notes remain clear.
- Foreground-lift text feels premium only when the foreground mask is clean and
  the text becomes readable quickly after emerging.

## Timing Rules

- Lock speech timing before export/re-import effects that bake text or VFX into a
  prepared base.
- Slide-on photos can use around 1.0-1.4 seconds for calm professional movement;
  shorten only after phone-size readability holds.
- Chapter cards should enter, settle for reading, and clear before the next body
  line needs attention.
- Tracked stickers should remain only for the emphasis beat and clear before they
  become clutter.
- VFX SFX should be placed after the visual impact frame is locked.
- Eye or power overlays should reveal, lock, or clear on the intended beat after
  the speed adjustment is approved.
- Switch-focus and parallax composites should be timed after the cutout source,
  title, and narration beat are chosen.

## Motion Rules

- For behind-subject effects, align the original clean clip exactly over the
  prepared base before applying Remove Background.
- For foreground-lift text, place text between duplicate video layers and keyframe
  it upward from behind the masked foreground only after the top cutout edge is
  approved.
- For eye/power overlays, align scale, position, mask/feather, and timing across
  the full movement range, not only the most flattering still frame.
- Use very short, bold text for video-in-text reveals; zoom through a readable
  letter or cut before the title overstays.
- Use the direct Text Mask method for video-in-text when no text animation is
  needed; use the compound/blend method when editable text animation matters.
- For outline reveal text, keep fill, outline, stroke, matte, and foreground
  cutout layers organized so chroma key and edge QC stay understandable.
- Film talking heads with rule-of-thirds negative space when photo overlays or
  side graphics will be added.
- For PiP borders, create the border slightly larger than the masked camera feed
  and move them together if animated.
- For split-mask illusions, align the mask to a real object edge and avoid
  crossing the boundary in ways that reveal the trick.
- For documentary depth, keep foreground cutouts, background, and text in
  separate compounds or layers so their motion intensity can differ.
- For filmed-screen texture, blend and lower texture opacity until UI, source
  text, and captions remain readable.

## Sound Rules

- Music and SFX from CapCut or external libraries still need license/platform
  status before delivery.
- Use footsteps, door hits, cloth, or room tone to sell clone/split-mask tricks
  only after the visual timing works.
- Use eye, zap, power, or identity cues only when the overlay reveal is visible
  and the cue lands on the lock/reveal frame.
- Keep hits, whooshes, explosions, and sticker pops below speech unless the
  section is intentionally music-led.

## Caption Rules

- Keep captions outside subject-sandwich edges, PiP regions, tracked stickers,
  and VFX impact areas.
- Keep captions away from the face/eye during small-detail transformation beats.
- Keep duplicate captions visible or repositioned during video-in-text portals,
  chapter cards, or full-screen text if designed text already carries the words;
  suppress only with explicit user approval.
- Restore ordinary captions immediately after the designed text section clears.

## Color Rules

- Match foreground cutout, base layer, and VFX layer so export/re-import passes
  do not reveal color or compression differences.
- Lower or grade VFX brightness when the keyed effect looks pasted on.
- Match eye/power overlays for glow and contrast so they do not erase the
  character expression.
- Chapter card colors and gradients must keep title text readable and match the
  project style.
- Archival or documentary collage grades should unify cutouts without hiding
  mask edges, title words, or source labels.

## Tool Implementation Notes

- CapCut: use overlays, Cutout/Remove Background, Chroma Key, Mask/Split, Camera
  Tracking, sticker Tracking, simple in/out animations, and export/re-import only
  when the layer order requires a prepared base.
- CapCut text-behind-object: duplicate the video, put text between the copies,
  apply Remove Background or Custom Removal to the top copy, and start Feather
  and Expansion around 5 only if it improves the edge.
- CapCut foreground-lift text: brush/select the foreground object on the top copy,
  put text below that cutout, set a Transform keyframe while hidden, move ahead,
  and raise the text until enough of the word clears to read.
- CapCut video-in-text: use Video > Mask > Text Mask for the fast version, or
  compound black background plus editable text, blend in the fill video, compound
  again, and set the wrapper to Screen for an animation-friendly version.
- CapCut eye overlay: speed-adjust first, place the overlay above the clip,
  scale/position/mask it, preview for drift, then export/re-import only after
  duration and beat alignment are stable.
- Premiere: use nests, Essential Graphics, Ultra Key, opacity masks, track mattes,
  guides, and duplicated foreground layers; use After Effects Roto Brush for
  harder subject sandwiches.
- Remotion: store the effect as data: layer order, render pass, matte color,
  chroma tolerance, tracking target, split-mask line, safe regions, and license
  status.
- ffmpeg: compose base, matte, VFX, foreground alpha, overlay paths, and audio
  cue frames explicitly; generate contact sheets for edges and collisions.
- Blender: use compositor masks, alpha/green-screen keying, image/video planes,
  and locked camera composites for more controlled renders.
- CapCut documentary depth: use Remove Background, rectangle masks with feather,
  compounds, transform keyframes, Graphs/Ease or Cubic Ease, opposite blur ramps,
  reduced background motion, and source/license notes for every cutout or texture.

## Common Mistakes

- Using auto cutout, tracking, or chroma key without checking the full affected
  range.
- Filming clone or split-mask tricks with auto exposure, auto focus, or camera
  movement.
- Adding photo overlays after filming a centered talking head with no screen
  space.
- Using long phrases or thin fonts for video-in-text reveals.
- Using text masks, outline reveals, or subject sandwiches with messy cutout
  edges, chroma specks, or muddy blend modes.
- Keyframing text from behind a foreground object before testing whether the brush
  edge holds through the move.
- Screen-recording or downloading VFX/music/SFX and treating availability as
  permission.
- Baking captions or final SFX into an export/re-import pass that still needs
  top-level changes.
- Building a small-detail overlay before the speed, beat, and duration are locked.
- Treating auto-removed documentary cutouts as finished without edge, source, or
  rights review.
- Pasting the same motion onto foreground, background, and text layers so no
  parallax depth remains.

## QC Checklist

- Layer order is correct: base, behind element or VFX, foreground cutout when
  needed.
- Foreground cutout edges stay clean through hair, hands, motion blur, and turns.
- Chroma key removes the matte color without edge specks, spill, or erased detail.
- Video-in-text and outline reveal effects preserve text readability and clean
  key/blend edges.
- Foreground-lift text reveals have clean mask edges throughout the full upward
  move and the word clears long enough to read.
- Tracking does not drift, jitter, or cover captions/proof.
- Photo stacks, PiP borders, and chapter cards avoid faces, captions, UI,
  products, proof, and active action.
- Split-mask/clone takes match in exposure, focus, white balance, and camera
  position.
- Music, SFX, stock, green-screen, sticker, and image assets have source/license
  notes before delivery.
- Eye/power overlays remain aligned through playback and have source/license status.
- Documentary cutout composites keep one primary focus, readable text, and clean
  edges through the full motion.
- Filmed-screen and archival texture layers preserve source context and captions.
