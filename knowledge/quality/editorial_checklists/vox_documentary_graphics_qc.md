# Vox-Style Documentary Graphics QC

Use this checklist before exporting editorial explainer graphics built from paper
textures, maps, charts, documents, screenshots, cutouts, and low-FPS motion.

## Rights And Style

- Style is inspired by editorial documentary grammar without copying a protected logo or exact publisher identity.
- Every map, screenshot, paper texture, photo, logo, SFX, and music track has source/license notes ready for the asset manifest.
- Official logos, institutional imagery, or public-figure cutouts do not imply endorsement unless the project has approval.

## Layout And Readability

- Paper/grid/background texture stays subordinate to text, charts, maps, and evidence.
- Typography has one clear primary focus per scene.
- Font roles are clear: title, utility label, source text, and emphasis text do not compete.
- Captions, labels, arrows, and title text do not compete for the same screen area.
- Text remains readable after warmth, opacity, blend modes, and FPS lag are applied.
- Line breaks, tracking, and line spacing keep source words, labels, and titles readable at final size.

## Maps And Data

- Map source is usable for the project and geography is not misleading.
- Route, outline, origin, destination, and labels are readable before the map exits.
- Chart values and source notes are known before animation.
- Bar lengths, labels, and axes represent the data honestly.

## Documents And Screenshots

- Highlight overlays sit behind or around source text without hiding wording.
- Enough source context remains visible to avoid cherry-picking.
- Private, sensitive, or uncleared details are removed or approved.
- Word-focus screenshot sequences reveal the repeated word and then restore full context.
- On-screen research overlays show real or approved process material, not generic
  fake research wallpaper.
- Research timelapses hold the discovery or proof frame long enough to understand.
- Filmed-screen texture keeps UI, cursor motion, source text, and captions readable.

## Depth, Cutouts, And Archival Treatment

- Switch-focus blur keeps at least one subject readable during the full handoff.
- Cutout masks and Remove Background edges stay clean after blur, scale, fade,
  FPS lag, and texture.
- Parallax foreground motion is stronger than background motion without revealing
  empty edges or cropping important labels.
- Title text layered behind or between cutouts remains readable at delivery size.
- Archival film frames, particles, fade, desaturation, and contrast preserve faces,
  source labels, dates, captions, and evidence.
- Generated, reconstructed, or illustrative images are labeled and not disguised
  as real archival proof.

## Motion And Timeline

- Dense sections are grouped into named compounds/nests/precomps and remain internally editable.
- Wipes, route traces, bars, highlights, and cutouts finish before the viewer needs the next claim.
- Eased/cubic curves are used on map pans, document moves, and compound zooms.
- Low-FPS/posterize texture is applied after timing lock and bypassed where it hurts readability.
- No compound transform reveals empty edges or crops important labels.
- Copied foreground motion is reduced before being applied to background layers so
  the scene keeps depth.
- Low-strength Pendulum or FPS Lag is removed if it makes title, caption, source,
  or cutout readability worse.

## Sound

- SFX cues land on visible entrances, highlights, swaps, route movement, or chart reveals.
- SFX stay below narration and are not placed on every small layer.
- Music is licensed/platform-safe or marked temp-only.
- Music exits at a natural phrase or crossfades cleanly.
- Research risers resolve into a visible discovery or claim and stay below narration.
- Projector, screen, ambience, or texture sounds are subtle and source/license safe.
