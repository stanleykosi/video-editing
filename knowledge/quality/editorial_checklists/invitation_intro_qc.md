# Invitation Intro QC

Use this checklist when a long-form documentary or essay delays the explicit
promise to build world, trust, and curiosity first.

## Opening Questions

- The first frame creates a specific question, tension, location clue, or sensory contradiction.
- The opening questions are nameable; they are not random confusion.
- At least one loop is closed early before more loops are added.
- Every loop opened in the intro is later closed, reframed, or intentionally escalated.

## Delayed Promise

- The explicit promise is delayed only while the edit builds world, conflict, tone, proof, or protagonist perspective.
- The call to adventure is clear before or as the promise arrives.
- The delayed promise is not used for short-form feed edits that need immediate premise clarity.
- A cold viewer can still tell what kind of video they are watching.

## Worldbuilding

- Wide shots establish scale and close-ups establish texture; neither is used as filler.
- Human-grounding moments are included when polished documentary footage risks feeling detached.
- Maps, proof screenshots, or evidence shots remain readable long enough to close trust loops.
- Visual contrast clearly defines the conflict.

## Motion And Treatment

- Letterbox, rotation, aspect, or color treatments have assigned story meaning.
- Reality/fantasy treatment is consistent, and any exception has a perspective reason.
- Movement-direction runs are cohesive before the edit breaks them.
- Digital zooms, rotations, and fast cuts do not hide evidence, faces, maps, or body language.

## Sound

- Sound cues characterize the world, material, conflict, or grounded reality.
- Pause beats carry meaning through image and sound rather than becoming dead air.
- Abrupt silence lands after a ramp and returns to a grounded image or natural sound.
- Music and SFX duck before VO or dialogue returns.
- Generic whooshes, impacts, or filler tracks are removed when they weaken the documentary tone.
