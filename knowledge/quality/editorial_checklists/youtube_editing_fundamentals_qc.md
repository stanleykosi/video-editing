# YouTube Editing Fundamentals QC

Use this checklist before calling a tutorial, talking-head, commentary, or short-form repurpose edit finished.

## Story And Pacing

- First frame creates a clear reason to continue.
- First 30 seconds match the title and thumbnail promise.
- Accidental opening silence, breath, camera adjustment, or hesitation is removed unless intentionally kept.
- In recap/commentary edits, every spoken character, person, place, object, product, UI element, proof point, or event is matched by the rendered picture at the viewer-facing timestamp.
- Named-subject visual handoffs start early enough that rounded player times do not show the previous subject.
- Movie recap/explainer edits use compact paragraph-sized narration units unless the user asked for a longer combined recap.
- No short-form dead air longer than 300 ms remains unless it creates tension, humor, emotion, or comprehension value.
- Rough cut is understandable before effects, captions, or SFX are added.
- In Premiere short-form workflows, silence removal and J-cut smoothing are complete before caption graphics or motion polish.
- Each B-roll, slide, screenshot, arrow, or overlay matches the spoken point it supports.
- The intro starts with viewer pain, desired result, proof, or tension before explaining the mechanism.
- The edit does not reveal the full payoff so early that curiosity disappears.
- Feed-first shorts communicate the claim, result, proof, or question within five seconds.
- Hook claims are supported by a later payoff or proof beat.

## Motion And Graphics

- Punch zooms or focus moves land within 2 frames of the spoken reference or beat.
- Focus zooms hold long enough for the viewer to read the target.
- Keyframed moves do not drift after they should be holding steady.
- Only one primary on-screen focus competes for attention at a time.
- Overlays, arrows, captions, and lower thirds do not collide.
- Text is readable at phone size over both bright and dark footage.
- Speaker tracking keeps the subject centered without cropping face, hands, captions, or proof.
- Visual-stack layers have clear hierarchy and clear before the next spoken concept.
- Hook text, filler cards, B-roll text, and full-screen object animations are chosen by line importance.
- Adjustment-layer zooms affect only intended sections and preserve caption/proof safety.
- B-roll text remains readable without hiding the story detail underneath.

## Sound

- Dialogue, music, and SFX are on controllable tracks or layers.
- Movie recap source audio is muted unless the user explicitly asked for a source-audio moment.
- No audible peak clips or hits red in the final mix.
- Music ducks before important words begin.
- Quiet story-critical words are repaired with gain or keyframes.
- Repeated whooshes or hits use variation rather than the same sound every time.
- SFX peaks do not overpower dialogue.
- Music tone matches the scene pace and emotion; it is not used to hide slow pacing.
- Social music candidates are auditioned under the finished edit and documented for tone fit and license/platform safety.
- Reverse hits build into real questions, reveals, or transitions.
- Whooshes and hits stay low enough that speech remains dominant.

## Captions And Shorts

- Captions match the audible speech timing.
- Captions do not cover faces, titles, cards, proof visuals, or the action being discussed.
- One- or two-word subtitle chunks are used only when they improve pace and readability.
- Duplicate captions remain visible, repositioned, simplified, or explicitly approved for suppression where visual text already explains the idea.
- AI-generated captions are corrected for wording, emphasis, line breaks, and filler-word clutter.
- Premiere caption graphics are created only after auto-caption wording, timing, chunking, and placement are corrected.
- Pop-fade caption motion finishes before the word needs to be read.
- AI-selected short clips make sense without the original long-form context.
- AI short start and end points do not cut off premise, setup, or payoff.
- Vertical layouts keep the active speaker, reactions, and proof visible.
- Timestamped contact sheets from the rendered preview or final have been reviewed around sensitive narration/visual handoffs and any user-reported timestamp.
- Movie recap visuals are transformed with short fragments, selective deletion/compression, safe mirror/flip choices, and speed fitting where needed; no long raw source run remains by accident.

## Export

- Export range is the intended full sequence or selected clip.
- Resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate, and filename match the delivery target.
- Final render has no black frames, offline media, missing fonts, caption collisions, or accidental watermark/overlay.
- The promoted final file passes spot checks for all previously reported timing/subject mismatches.
